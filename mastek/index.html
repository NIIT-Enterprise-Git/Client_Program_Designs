01

Why Forward Deployed Engineering, and why now

The talent gap behind stalled enterprise AI

Enterprise AI ambitions are becoming clearer. The execution path is not. Many organisations can identify promising use cases, select platforms and launch pilots. Far fewer can move solutions into production, embed them into workflows and prove business value. Fragmented data, legacy systems, weak governance, unclear ownership and limited user adoption all get in the way. Traditional delivery models are built around scope, milestones and utilisation. They are not designed to carry accountability from business aspiration through production deployment, adoption and measurable impact.

Forward Deployed Engineering offers a different delivery model, running from intent to impact. FDEs work where customer context, business priorities, product thinking, AI engineering, architecture, change and value realisation meet. They do not stop at prototypes. The result is not more experimentation. It is working, secure, scalable and adopted intelligent systems.

Three roles in one professional

The Engineer

Ships production-grade agents, integrations and guardrails inside the customer's stack.

The Consultant

Finds the real problem behind the request and speaks the customer's language.

The Owner

Accountable for adoption and measurable value, not for closing tickets.

AI changes the delivery model itself. An AI-centric application depends on models, enterprise data, prompts, context, APIs, agents, guardrails and user feedback, and its behaviour keeps changing. It cannot be engineered, packaged and released like traditional software. It must be delivered as continuous intelligence: experimented, validated beyond functional correctness, rolled out in phases and monitored for behaviour, data quality and business outcomes.
02

Program Objectives
  • Build a sustainable, funnel-based FDE talent pipeline for Mastek across three maturity levels: Associate, Staff and Lead FDE.
  • Shift engineers from requirements-first software delivery to outcome-first problem discovery, where ambiguity is part of the job rather than a blocker.
  • Develop balanced capability across Consultative Excellence, Enterprise Operating Context and Solution Realization, weighted to each level's role.
  • Build hands-on capability to take AI solutions from PoC to production: RAG, agents, integration, evaluation, guardrails, observability, token economics and AI security.
  • Develop the softer competencies that give FDEs their edge: business and financial acumen, stakeholder influence, executive communication and execution rigour.
  • Certify readiness on demonstrated performance in realistic FDE missions, assessed by an independent panel, rather than on attendance.

Case Method & Mentoring

From ambiguity to an iterative AI solution

The case study is the integrating spine of the program: an end-to-end FDE engagement simulation, not a classroom exercise. Participants start with an incomplete, unstructured customer situation and uncover the solution through questioning, experimentation, implementation, feedback and iteration.

Active Mentoring Throughout the Journey

Mentors do not give answers
What mentors do
  • Challenge thinking and question assumptions.
  • Expose weak spots and enterprise constraints.
  • Facilitate deeper reflection and decision quality.
  • Guide with experience and diverse perspective.
  • Support iterative and continuous improvement.
  • Simulate stakeholder objections and help teams interpret failure.
Key mindset shifts
  • Accept ambiguity and navigate instead of waiting for complete requirements.
  • Validate assumptions early with prototypes and experiments.
  • Deliver small increments and learn from feedback.
  • Continuously refine the solution based on business outcomes and user insights.
  • Balance speed with technical quality and measurable value.

Sample Progressive Mission Case

AI-native garment supply chain · three levels of depth
FDE Mission Case

AI-Native Garment Supply Chain

Right Style. Right Size. Right Store. Right Season.

Build an AI-native supply chain that improves customer loyalty by ensuring the right style, size and colour are available at the right store and season, without increasing inventory disproportionately. Translate demand, inventory and customer signals into timely replenishment, allocation and rebalancing decisions.

A major global garment retailer wants customer loyalty to become a measurable supply-chain outcome. The FDE must translate the sponsor's direction into an executable AI adoption mission, working hands-on with business teams, aspiring FDEs and specialist engineers.

Non-negotiable outcomes

  • Improve availability at SKU-size-colour level and reduce avoidable "could not find it" journeys.
  • Increase full-price sell-through and reduce excess stock and markdowns.
  • Shorten the time from demand signal to operational response.
  • Recommendations and automated actions must be explainable, observable and governed.

Reflective questions

Which workflow should be tackled first to prove meaningful value?
What value measures and acceptance criteria show the pilot is working?
Which tasks can aspiring FDEs own safely?
Where should AI recommend, where may it act, and where must a human decide?
What prototype can test the most important assumption quickly?
What must be reusable after the mission?

Carried forward from Level 1: the problem is not simply forecasting, and the answer is not more inventory everywhere. The mission is dependable availability at the point of customer intent while protecting margin and working capital.

Enterprise situation introduced

ERP, POS, e-commerce, OMS, WMS, transport, loyalty, promotions, returns and supplier platforms built at different times. Some regions have RFID and near-real-time stock visibility; others rely on cycle counts and batch updates. Product, size, colour and inventory-state definitions are not consistently governed. A stock record may include items that are reserved, damaged, misplaced, returned or already promised online.

FDE task at this level: define the first production slice, decompose it into workflows and use cases, set acceptance criteria, expose assumptions, coordinate specialists and keep the mission tied to value.

Carried forward from Level 2: a controlled in-season availability pilot for selected categories, stores and channels, judged on customer availability and commercial outcomes, not model accuracy alone.

What the FDE now owns

  • Outcome and OKR integrity: avoid false wins such as availability bought with excess stock.
  • Critical-path dependency management: product-master clean-up, RFID coverage, OMS releases, security approvals.
  • Evaluation, guardrails, fallback behaviour and escalation when feeds are stale or confidence is low.
  • Adoption as part of engineering: overrides captured, trust earned through use.
  • Value realisation against baseline, with confounders such as promotions and seasonality accounted for.

Discussion challenge

The pilot is technically functional but results are mixed. Availability has improved in some stores, markdown exposure has increased in others, several planners override recommendations, one inventory feed is unreliable and a parallel OMS release is two weeks late. What do you continue, change, pause, escalate and measure next?

For Mastek, the mission case is co-created around Mastek's own domains, customer scenarios and preferred technology stack.

04

Outcome-driven Thinking

Start from the metric, then work back to the model
1 · Outcome
Which number must move?

Resolve disputes in [__] days, not [__].

2 · Decision
Which decision gets faster or better?

Triage and routing of each new dispute.

3 · Data
What signal does that decision need?

Complaint text plus the transaction record.

4 · System
Only now: what do we build?

Extraction agent with confidence-based routing.

The question changes from "which model should we use?" to "which number moves, and who signs off on it?"

Assessment & Certification

What makes an FDE ready? The NIIT Standard

Certification rests on evidence, not participation. Training and certification are kept separate, and readiness is judged on demonstrated performance in real or standardised simulated FDE missions.

01

Six Principles
Defined standard

Observable competencies and proficiency levels define FDE readiness.

Evidence, not participation

Certification based on demonstrated performance in real or simulated FDE missions.

Independent panel

Neutral, multi-disciplinary assessors. Training and certification are kept separate.

Calibrated assessment

Common rubric, assessor calibration, scoring anchors and consistent pass/fail rules.

Engineering credibility

Technical readiness validated by current practitioners; business and executive readiness by the broader panel.

Governed and validated

Conflict-of-interest rules, documented governance, pilot certification and later validation against on-the-job performance.

AI-enabled Learning Platform

It assesses people, rather than simply delivering content to them
Pre-assessment

Establishes the readiness baseline across technical fluency, AI fluency and consulting readiness, and sets each participant's entry point.

AI role plays

Simulated customer and stakeholder conversations. Participants handle a live exchange and are scored on how they handle it, not on what they recall.

Scenario simulations

Ambiguous situations that evaluate communication, technical judgement and problem solving together, the way they occur in real work.

04

Domain Case Assessment Method

Assessing forward-deployed behaviour, not recall

The case assessment avoids testing definitions, memorised frameworks or product trivia. It uses realistic domain situations with incomplete information and competing stakeholder priorities, allows multiple defensible approaches, and scores the quality of reasoning and trade-offs rather than architecture conformity.

Stage 1 · Case presentation
  • Group receives one domain case in advance.
  • 10-minute solution presentation; assumptions stated explicitly.
  • Covers the 3–5 questions they would ask the customer, approach, architecture, data, NFRs, first production slice, and success metrics.
Stage 2 · Panel probing
  • 15–20 minutes of probing with new information introduced.
  • Strong groups revise their approach without becoming defensive or over-committing.
  • Sample probe: "Risk says no customer decision can be made by an LLM. Does your solution still work?"
05

Measuring Program Impact
Role readiness
  • Pre vs post improvement on the same three-pillar rubric.
  • Quality of artefacts and decisions in simulations and showcases.
  • Manager and mentor confidence to deploy the participant on a customer mission.
Field effectiveness
  • PoC/pilot-to-production conversion and time to production.
  • Time to first meaningful user adoption.
  • Achievement of mission KPIs; fewer late governance surprises.
Enterprise leverage
  • Reusable patterns, playbooks and reference assets created from field work.
  • Better use of scarce specialist capacity.
  • Evidence-backed next use cases in the AI pipeline.
Proven in Delivery

Three FDE programs NIIT is delivering today

NIIT is currently running FDE capability programs for three clients. Each was co-created for a different starting talent pool and target role. Client names are withheld; what matters for Mastek is what each design does distinctively. Click a program for detail.

Program A · Lead Forward Deployed Engineer

Design outcome: not engineers who are simply better at building AI systems, but Lead FDEs who understand the customer problem, make pragmatic decisions under ambiguity, rapidly validate hypotheses, mobilise the right capabilities and defend a path to measurable value.

Phase 0 · 2 hrsCalibration assessment
Phase 1 · 40 hrsGenAI & Agentic AI foundations (Full Stack + Data only)
Phase 2 · 4 hrsFDE readiness masterclass
Phase 3 · 44 hrsFDE specialisation & simulation
Distinctive design choices
  • Persona-specific entry: Agentic AI engineers skip Phase 1 after prerequisite validation (50 hrs vs 90 hrs).
  • Calibration assessment weighted 70% tech stack / 30% AI-assisted engineering, plus optional three-pillar FDE calibration.
  • Specialisation built on simulations: customer discovery, architecture trade-off challenge, rapid prototype sprint, FDE pod simulation, production-readiness review, delivery war-room and executive steering committee.
  • Capstone case revealed progressively with inject cards; complexity tuned to the calibration findings.
Assessment approach
  • Domain case assessment across seven areas, from business problem understanding to business outcomes and value.
  • Panel probing questions withheld from candidates to test adaptability.
  • Individual Lead FDE development plan from panel feedback.
What Mastek's design takes from it: the Orientation readiness calibration, the Unlearn · Learn · Relearn mindset bridge, and simulation-based Introspects with injects.

Program B · Emerging FDE: AI Solution Architect

For solution architects, tech leads and senior developers moving into consulting-oriented FDE roles, with strong Python, distributed systems, cloud and data pipeline prerequisites.

P3AI architecture & system design
P4RAG & data pipelines

P7Observability & reliability

Distinctive design choices
  • Every module ends in a hands-on outcome, from a prompt library to a multi-agent workflow to a "fix the broken pipeline" exercise.
  • AI decision framework: when to use RAG, fine-tuning, agents or classic ML, with cost, latency and maintainability trade-offs.
  • Multi-cloud deployment (AWS and Azure), DevOps for AI, blue-green and canary releases, and performance tuning.
  • Formative assessments and review-refactor cycles built into the phase sequence.
Consulting woven through
  • Client discovery role-play, use-case prioritisation model and enterprise AI readiness workshop.
  • Client architecture pitch role-play and ROI case for the AI solution.
  • Capstone runs Discovery → Architecture → Implementation → Demo.
What Mastek's design takes from it: build depth in the Deploy phase (RAG, agents, evaluation, observability, troubleshooting) and the hands-on outcome required of every session.

Program C · Customer-facing Solution Engineer

A two-tier program. The junior tier (1–7 years) builds customer-facing engineering execution; the senior tier (5–10+ years) builds technical leadership of customer engagements.

02

Optional Extension: Customer Success FDE

For customer-facing, non-engineering talent

NIIT also offers a Customer Success FDE intervention (around 30–43 hours blended, followed by a coached field mission) that turns experienced customer-facing professionals into AI adoption leaders who own the thread from customer intent to measurable value. It does not try to make them AI engineers. On complex missions, the Customer Success FDE and a Lead or Technical FDE work as a paired field leadership model: one protecting customer intent, adoption and value continuity, the other protecting technical integrity and production quality.

Relevance for Mastek: an optional companion track for account and delivery leaders, so FDE pods have strong customer-side orchestration alongside engineering depth.
